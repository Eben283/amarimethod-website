In a session

He watches how your body responds.

You spend the session on your feet. Garrett sets up props, gymnastic rings to hold, blocks to move over, positions where you hang and let your body open, and guides you through them while he watches how you respond. He isn't doing the work to you. He's showing you where your body is holding, and you're the one who moves into the release.

The work usually starts with pain. A neck that won't turn, a back that keeps flaring. That part tends to shift fast. What comes after is the deeper part of the method. Your nervous system settles, your breath drops lower, and things your body has been holding for years start to move. Sessions can get emotional, and Garrett treats that as part of the work rather than a detour from it.

Named for a boy whose story changed everything.

Garrett knows what it feels like when a body turns a corner. As a kid he spent nights in hospital oxygen tents with severe asthma. Then his mother took him to a practitioner, and over the following months he came off the medication and started breathing on his own again. That experience is why he went on to study the body professionally.

Years later, in his final year of training, a woman named Phyllis told him her 11-year-old grandson had just died from complications of asthma. His name was Amari.

Amari was the same age Garrett had been when that kind of care changed his life. He kept coming back to one question: What if I could have helped him?

//
In that moment, Garrett made a promise to Amari and to his own younger self. He would spend the rest of his working life teaching people what saved him, and keep it within reach of anyone willing to do the work.
Garrett Hewstan
Why “Amari”

Every person who moves through this work carries his name forward.

Garrett named the method after Amari so the promise would stay attached to the work. It isn't a brand name he picked from a list. It's a reminder of who the work is for.

Twenty-five years on, that promise still shapes how he shows up for every client who walks in.

What makes the method different.

Four things about how sessions work, and why Garrett set them up that way after 25 years inside other approaches.

01

You learn to change your own state.

The pain quieting down is the front door. The method underneath is about access: you learn to reach the places where your body braces, and to shift your own state instead of waiting for it to change. It takes longer than the first-session relief, and it is what the series is built around.

02

The work happens off the table, on your feet.

Garrett spent years guiding gateway work with clients lying down. He kept seeing that the real shift happened when the client moved, so he rebuilt the work standing up. You hold rings or hang, you move over blocks, and Garrett guides you through what he sees in how you respond.

03

Nothing gets cracked or adjusted.

There are no drugs and no surgery. Your own movement does the reorganizing. You don't come back forever, and the home practice is about five minutes a day on your living room floor.

04

If you don't feel a difference, we keep working.

If you don't experience noticeable relief after your first session, we keep working with you until you do, at no additional charge.
