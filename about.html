Named for a boy who inspired change.

Garrett felt an immediate connection to Amari, a boy he never met. Both faced life-threatening complications from asthma at the same age; Garrett survived, while Amari did not. Amari's passing gave Garrett's work deeper meaning and purpose: to remind people that, with the right guidance, the body has a remarkable capacity to heal—and that care should move people toward self-empowerment, not dependence.

What makes the Amari Method different.

  1. 01

    Your relationship with your body changes.

    Instead of fighting your body, you begin listening to it with curiosity. You stop telling your body what to do and start letting your body show you what it needs. The method is about learning an entirely different somatic language.

  2. 02

    Sessions are dynamic.

    You are guided through the protocol your body needs in that moment. You move around the room: some protocols take place on the floor, while others use gymnastic rings or stairs. You are never confined to a treatment table.

  3. 03

    Nothing gets cracked or adjusted.

    There are no drugs and no surgery. Your own movement does the reorganizing. You don't come back forever, and the home practice is about five minutes a day on your living room floor.

  4. 04

    If you don't feel a difference, we keep working.

    If you don't experience noticeable relief after your first session, we keep working with you until you do, at no additional charge.
