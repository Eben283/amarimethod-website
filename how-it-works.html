What Is the Amari Method?

The Amari Method is built on a simple idea: you're in pain because your body is out of balance. Something is working too hard because something else isn't working enough. Dr. Garrett Hewstan created 8 core protocols that identify where that imbalance is and teach you how to correct it yourself, using simple props like yoga blocks, foam rollers, and gymnastic rings. The protocols evolve as your body changes, and you do them at home between sessions. Most clients feel a difference in their first session.

Over 51 million U.S. adults live with chronic pain, and low back pain is the leading cause of disability worldwide. The Amari Method was built for people who have tried other approaches and are still hurting.

Sources: CDC MMWR 2023 · Lancet Rheumatology 2023

Why Other Approaches Haven't Worked

Your body wants balance, not just pain relief. Most treatments address the symptom without asking why it's there.

Chiropractors

Adjustments can provide real relief, but they're doing the work for you. Without learning what's pulling your body out of balance in the first place, you keep coming back for more adjustments.

The difference: The Amari Method teaches you to correct the imbalance yourself so you don't need ongoing visits.

Physical Therapy

PT prescribes exercises, but they're often generic to your diagnosis, not specific to your body. And they tend to focus only on muscles when the issue might be in your joints or bones.

The difference: Dr. Garrett assesses whether your tension is active or passive and adapts each protocol to what your body actually needs.

Painkillers

Pain is your body telling you something is out of balance. Medication silences that message without addressing the cause. The underlying imbalance continues to get worse.

The difference: The Amari Method addresses the cause of the pain, not just the sensation.

What a Session Looks Like

There are 8 core protocols in the Amari Method. You learn them at your own pace over a series of sessions. Here's how the process works.

1

Discovery

Your first session starts with an assessment. Dr. Garrett watches how your body moves and determines whether your tension is active (muscles gripping too hard) or passive (tension in your joints and bones). This tells him which protocols your body needs and in what order.

Why this matters: Most approaches give everyone the same treatment. Dr. Garrett adapts everything to your specific body. Some people need to let go more, some people need to engage more. Getting this wrong makes things worse.
2

Guided Protocols

You work with simple props like yoga blocks, foam rollers, and gymnastic rings while Dr. Garrett guides you through each protocol. He adapts everything to what your body is doing that day. The protocols feel good while you do them, and most clients feel a difference in their first session.

What makes this different: You're not lying on a table while someone works on you. You're moving, standing, hanging. Your body does the work. Dr. Garrett guides you and sees things you can't see yourself.
3

Home Practice

You learn to do the protocols on your own at home. About five minutes a day. As your body changes, the protocols evolve with you. Follow-up sessions with Dr. Garrett refine your practice and introduce new protocols as you're ready for them.

Why people stick with it: Unlike PT exercises that feel like homework, these protocols feel good while you do them. That's by design. If it doesn't feel good, your body doesn't want it right now.
4

Ongoing Practice

Once you've learned the protocols, they're yours for life. Many clients continue coming back for periodic sessions because the work keeps getting deeper, but you're never dependent on appointments. You have the tools to maintain your own balance.

The goal: You become your own healer. Dr. Garrett's role is to guide you there.

Conditions We Work With

The root cause is usually the same: your body is out of balance. Here's how that shows up in different areas.

Neck Pain →

Forward head posture and thoracic stiffness drive most chronic neck pain. We identify and correct the structural pattern — not just the neck.

Learn about neck pain treatment

Shoulder Pain →

Rotator cuff issues and impingement are almost always positioning problems. Correcting scapular mechanics resolves most shoulder pain without surgery.

Learn about shoulder pain treatment

Hip Pain →

Hip impingement, bursitis, and tight hip flexors are rooted in pelvic alignment. Restoring proper positioning eliminates the compression driving the pain.

Learn about hip pain treatment

Knee Pain →

Most knee pain starts in the hip. Correcting how force moves through the lower body — not treating the knee in isolation — is what creates lasting relief.

Learn about knee pain treatment

Lower Back Pain →

Lower back pain is almost always a pelvic and hip problem. Tight hip flexors, inhibited glutes, and anterior pelvic tilt load the lumbar spine constantly — until the pattern is corrected.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Amari Method for everyone?
+
The Amari Method works for most people with acute or chronic pain — low back, hip, shoulder, etc. During your free assessment, Dr. Garrett can determine if this is the right approach for your specific situation.

+
No. Try the Amari Method first. Most clients find relief without surgery. Many have avoided surgery entirely by discovering that alignment fixes their pain.
