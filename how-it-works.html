The principle

Your body can work differently.

Long hours, hard training, old injuries, and repetition can leave some parts of the body doing too much while other support is not fully participating. The place that hurts is real. It is not always the only place worth working with.

Amari works with the whole body in the moment. Position, leverage, breath, muscular engagement, touch, and small movements are brought together so your body can find a more available way to move.

The input can be subtle. The result does not have to be.

The assessment

One body can need more release. Another can need more support.

The Assessment begins by noticing how your body is working today, then matching the guidance to what is actually present.

When the body is working too hard

Some bodies need to let go.

They feel tight, contracted, and unable to settle. The work may create a setup where overworking can release and movement becomes less effortful.

When support has gone quiet

Other bodies need more participation.

They may feel unstable, heavy, or unable to find support. The work may ask long-unused parts of the body to participate again.

The point is not a label. It is finding the physical input your body can use now, rather than giving every person the same exercise or stretch.

The movement vocabulary

A growing practice, not a fixed curriculum.

Amari has a living library of movements and setups. What matters is not completing them all; it is learning what is relevant as your body changes.

FAQ

The questions everyone asks.

How many sessions does it take?

+

There is no preset number. The $29 Assessment lets you experience the work and understand what it may ask of your body. Some people come for a focused issue; others build an ongoing practice with Garrett as their body changes.

Can I do this virtually?

+

Some guidance can happen virtually, but the core Amari experience is in person: Garrett can see how you move and use hands-on support when it is useful. Ask about virtual options when you book.

I'm not flexible. Can I still do this?

+

Yes. You do not need flexibility or prior movement experience. The work begins with the body and capacity you have that day.

I've had surgery or have a serious diagnosis. Is this safe?

+

Tell us before you book. Some situations need medical guidance or clearance first, and Amari is not a replacement for medical evaluation or treatment. Garrett can help determine whether an Assessment is an appropriate next step.

How is this different from yoga or Pilates?

+

Yoga and Pilates can be excellent practices. Amari is one-on-one and hands-on: the session is built around the specific way your body is moving that day, rather than a shared class or prescribed sequence.

What if it doesn't work for me?

+

The Assessment lets you experience the work firsthand. You and Garrett will notice what changes, what does not, and what may be useful next.

A yoga block and a foam roller. That's it?

+

The tools are simple because the value is in the setup: where you are positioned, what you are asked to engage, where breath goes, and the real-time guidance around it.

How is this different from physical therapy?

+

Amari is not physical therapy and does not diagnose or treat medical conditions. It is private, hands-on guided movement for people who want to change how their body moves and feels. It can sit alongside care from licensed clinicians when appropriate.

I've tried everything. Why would this be different?

+

You do not have to believe in a theory before you come in. The Assessment gives you a concrete way to find out whether this particular kind of guided movement changes something for you.

Do I have to do something at home?

+

Not to begin. If you continue, Living Practice can help you revisit work you have already learned, but it is a support for the in-person practice—not a required generic program.
