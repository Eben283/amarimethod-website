The American Healthcare Crisis: A System Designed for Sickness

12 min read • February 2026 • By Dr. Garrett Hewstan

The American healthcare system is broken. Not broken in the sense of malfunctioning—it works exactly as designed. And that's the problem. It's designed for sickness, not health. It's designed to manage symptoms, not cure disease. It's designed to maximize profit, not maximize wellness. The evidence is overwhelming, and yet we've been conditioned to accept this dystopian reality as normal.

The System's Core Failure: Treating Symptoms, Not Causes

Walk into any doctor's office with chronic back pain, neck pain, or shoulder pain, and here's what happens: You get a diagnosis based on an imaging test. Then you're offered pharmaceutical solutions—pain medications, muscle relaxants, anti-inflammatories. If the pain persists, they escalate: cortisone injections, possibly surgery.

Nowhere in this process is anyone asking: Why do you have this pain? What's the root cause? Is it postural collapse? Spinal misalignment? A lifestyle that's created cascading dysfunction in your body?

The pharmaceutical model cannot ask these questions. Asking "why?" would destroy the profit model. If doctors identified root causes—poor posture, desk work alignment, daily habits—they'd have to say: "Fix your posture. Align your spine. Strengthen your stabilizer muscles." That doesn't sell pills.

Instead, the system offers endless symptom management. Take this pill. When it stops working, take two pills. When side effects develop from the first pill, take another pill to counteract them. This is the pharmaceutical treadmill, and it's incredibly profitable.

The Pharmaceutical Industry's Perverse Incentive Structure

Here's the uncomfortable truth: Pharmaceutical companies profit from sickness, not health. They don't profit from cured patients—cured patients don't need their products. They profit from dependent patients. Patients on long-term medications. Patients who'll buy their pills for decades.

This creates a perverse incentive structure:

  • Cure = No Profit: If a drug cured disease, that patient would no longer need the drug. Revenue ends.
  • Symptom Management = Lifetime Revenue: Managing symptoms indefinitely creates lifetime customers.
  • Marketing Over Research: Pharmaceutical companies spend more on marketing than research. Why? Because convincing people they need drugs is more profitable than developing cures.
  • Influencing Doctors: Pharmaceutical reps, speaking fees, and research funding influence what doctors prescribe. It's not always about what's best for patients—it's about what's most profitable.

The system isn't broken. It's working perfectly for everyone except patients.

The Doctor's Dilemma

Most doctors aren't villains. They genuinely want to help people. But they're trapped in a system that limits their options:

  • Time constraints: Doctors have 15 minutes per patient. There's no time to investigate root causes.
  • Training limitations: Medical school teaches pharmaceutical and surgical interventions, not lifestyle and alignment-based solutions.
  • Insurance structure: Insurance reimburses procedures and pills, not preventive alignment work or lifestyle coaching.
  • Liability concerns: Recommending something outside the pharmaceutical-surgical paradigm feels risky, even if it might be more effective.

The system constrains doctors to operate within it, even when they know there are better ways.

The Rising Tide of Natural Health Practitioners

This is why natural health practitioners are gaining ground. Not because they're marketing better—they're not. But because they're actually solving problems.

When someone has chronic back pain and finds relief through body alignment therapy, they tell everyone. When someone's been on pain medication for years and finally gets off it through postural correction and proper alignment, that word spreads. People are desperate for solutions that actually work.

Natural health practitioners ask the right question: Why? Why does this pain exist? What's causing it? And the answers are usually simple: misalignment, postural collapse, weakness in stabilizer muscles, habits that reinforce dysfunction.

The solutions are also simple: fix the alignment, restore proper posture, strengthen the right muscles, create new habits. The body heals. The pain disappears. The person doesn't need ongoing medications or repeated procedures.

The Evidence We Ignore

There's substantial evidence that conservative, alignment-based approaches work better than pharmaceutical interventions for many chronic pain conditions. Yet this evidence is routinely ignored:

  • Back pain: Studies show that spinal manipulation and postural correction outperform pain medications for most back pain cases.
  • Neck pain: Physical therapy and alignment work consistently outperform cervical collars and NSAIDs.
  • Shoulder pain: Posture correction and scapular stabilization resolve most rotator cuff issues without surgery.
  • Drug dependency: Opioid medications have created an addiction crisis, yet pain pill prescriptions continue to rise.

The evidence is clear. But it conflicts with the system's profit model, so it's marginalized. Meanwhile, people suffer on medications that don't address their root problem.

What Would a Rational Healthcare System Look Like?

If the United States actually cared about health (as opposed to profit), the healthcare system would look different:

  • Prevention First: Teach postural alignment in schools. Make body mechanics a foundational skill. Prevent problems before they start.
  • Root Cause Investigation: When someone has pain, investigate and address the cause, not the symptom.
  • Conservative First: Try alignment, posture, and lifestyle changes before pills. Try pills before surgery. Try everything else before invasive procedures.
  • Alignment Specialists: Natural health practitioners wouldn't be "alternative"—they'd be primary care. Finding and fixing misalignment would be the foundation of healthcare.
  • Incentives Realigned: Doctors and practitioners would be paid for outcomes, not for procedures. Cure patients, get paid. Keep them dependent on medications, lose money.

This would be a system designed for health. But it would be terrible for pharmaceutical company profits, and we can't have that.

The Choice Before Us

We have a choice. We can continue accepting a healthcare system designed for sickness—one that manages symptoms, creates dependency, enriches pharmaceutical companies, and leaves people suffering. Or we can demand better.

That means recognizing that natural health practitioners—chiropractors, alignment therapists, physical therapists, wellness coaches—aren't "alternative." They're often the solution that the mainstream system refuses to prioritize.

It means taking responsibility for our own health through proper alignment, posture, and daily practices that support our body's natural healing capacity.

It means supporting practitioners who ask "why?" instead of just prescribing pills.

The healthcare crisis won't be solved by the system that created it. It will be solved by patients choosing better alternatives, by practitioners who refuse to participate in symptom management, and by a fundamental shift toward alignment-based, root-cause healthcare.

Your body wants to be healthy. It's asking for alignment, not pills. It's asking for a system that supports health, not profits.

Are you ready to listen?

FAQ: Healthcare System & Natural Health

Is the healthcare system really designed for sickness?

The structure creates incentives for symptom management over curing. When pharmaceutical companies profit from ongoing medication use and doctors are compensated for procedures, the system naturally prioritizes these approaches over root-cause solutions that would eliminate the need for ongoing treatment.

Are natural health practitioners qualified?

Many are highly qualified, with extensive training in anatomy, biomechanics, and therapeutic techniques. The distinction between "conventional" and "natural" shouldn't be seen as "qualified" vs. "unqualified"—but rather as different approaches to healthcare.

Can alignment therapy replace medication?

For many pain conditions, alignment therapy addresses the root cause, making ongoing medication unnecessary. For other conditions, a combination approach works best. The key is finding the root cause rather than just managing symptoms indefinitely.

Why don't more doctors recommend alignment-based approaches?

Training, time constraints, insurance structure, and system incentives all play a role. Many doctors would recommend root-cause approaches if the system allowed for it. Some do—but they often operate outside the pharmaceutical-surgical paradigm.

Is surgery ever necessary for chronic pain?

In some cases, yes. Severe disc herniation, significant structural damage, or certain neurological conditions may require surgery. But these cases are far rarer than the number of surgeries performed. Many pain surgeries could be prevented through early intervention with alignment therapy.

How do I know if alignment therapy will help my pain?

If your pain stems from postural dysfunction, spinal misalignment, or movement pattern dysfunction, alignment therapy is highly likely to help. The best way to find out is through a professional assessment with a qualified alignment specialist.

Is it too late if I've had chronic pain for years?

No. The body's capacity for healing doesn't expire. Even pain that's lasted for decades typically resolves once the underlying misalignment is corrected and proper patterns are restored.

What should I do if my doctor won't support alternative approaches?

Find a doctor who will. Or work with natural health practitioners in parallel with conventional care. You don't have to choose one or the other—you can use both, prioritizing what works. Your health is too important to be constrained by system politics.
