Why it keeps hurting

The ache is in your neck. The cause has been building for years.

Your neck muscles are working overtime to hold your head in position because something below them has shifted. When that support goes quiet, your neck is left doing a job it was never built for.

01

Ten pounds a day, every inch forward

Your neck is working overtime because your mid-back has rounded forward and your shoulder blades have drifted out of place. For every inch your head sits in front of your shoulders, it adds roughly ten pounds of load on your neck. Most people are two to three inches forward without realizing it.

02

Why stretching and massage keep wearing off

Releasing the tight muscles in your neck and upper traps feels good in the moment. But those muscles are tight because they're overworking. If what they're compensating for never changes, they tighten right back up within a day or two.

03

Why relief keeps needing a repeat visit

Manual therapy and hands-on work can bring real relief. But if the pattern pulling your head forward hasn't changed, the load returns on its own schedule. Garrett works both ends of the problem at once. What's overworking releases. What went quiet wakes back up.

I've had neck pain for years. Can anything still help?

+

Chronic neck pain, even decades-long, almost always has a pattern that can be improved. The muscles and joints may have been out of position for a long time, but the underlying pattern is still correctable. Many of Garrett's practice members had been managing neck pain for years before someone finally looked at what was driving it from below the neck. How long you've had pain doesn't predict the outcome. The pattern does.

Can neck pain cause headaches?

+

Yes. When the muscles at the base of your skull are overworking to hold your head up, they compress nerves and restrict blood flow. That's the most common cause of tension headaches and headaches that start at the back of the skull. Correcting the pattern that's overloading those muscles resolves the headaches alongside the neck pain.

Can neck pain cause shoulder pain or arm numbness?

+

Yes. Neck misalignment commonly causes pain that radiates into the shoulders and upper arms, and in some cases numbness or tingling down into the hands. When the cervical spine is compressed, it can irritate the nerves that run into the arm. Addressing the pattern creating the compression reduces these secondary symptoms too.

I sit at a desk all day. Is that causing my neck pain?

+

Almost certainly contributing to it. Prolonged desk work rounds the mid-back, collapses the shoulder blades forward, and pushes your head into a forward position. The issue isn't sitting itself. It's what sitting does to your body over time. The protocols in the Amari Method are designed to counteract the effects of desk work and restore what sitting disrupts.

How is this different from other approaches?

+

Most approaches treat the neck in isolation. Cervical stretches, chin tucks, and strengthening routines address where the pain is, not where it's coming from. Garrett looks at the whole pattern: mid-back mobility, shoulder blade mechanics, and how your head is positioned relative to the rest of your body. The result is lasting change, not ongoing management.
