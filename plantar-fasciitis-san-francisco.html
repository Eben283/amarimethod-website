I've tried orthotics, a night splint, and rolling a ball. What's different here?

+

All three address the foot directly. They reduce symptoms without changing why the foot is being asked to absorb so much load. Orthotics support the arch but don't activate it. A night splint lengthens the fascia overnight but it tightens again the moment you weight-bear in the morning. Rolling helps for the moment. Garrett works on the calf, ankle, and hip mechanics that determine how force enters your foot, so the foot stops adapting in the first place.

Why is the pain worst first thing in the morning?

+

Your fascia tightens overnight while you're not weight-bearing. The first steps of the morning ask that tight fascia to suddenly absorb your full body weight. That's why the pain is sharp at first and eases as you walk around. The morning sharpness is a signal of how loaded the fascia is overall.

Do I need imaging before starting?

+

Usually no. Plantar fasciitis is typically identified from the pattern of pain and where it lives. Imaging matters if there's reason to suspect a stress fracture or a heel spur changing the picture, but most plantar fasciitis is mechanical, and that's what Garrett works on.

What about my running?

+

Running is one of the things that loads up plantar fasciitis. But stopping running rarely solves it because the underlying pattern is still there, your hip and ankle mechanics are still loading the foot the same way when you walk around the house. Most runners can keep running once the pattern changes, sometimes with a lighter volume for the first few weeks.
