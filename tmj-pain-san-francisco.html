Why Most TMJ Treatments Don't Last

They manage the symptom in your jaw — not the pattern driving it.

Night Guards

A guard protects your teeth from the force of clenching, but it does nothing to change why you clench in the first place. The muscle tension, the grinding, and the pain are all still there in the morning — you've just cushioned the damage.

Result: Protected teeth. Same jaw pain. Same clenching.

Botox & Muscle Relaxers

Injecting or medicating the jaw muscles can quiet them temporarily, but the forward head posture and muscle imbalance that overloaded them remains. As the effect fades, the same forces rebuild the same tension.

Result: Weeks of relief. Recurring tension. Repeat treatments.

Waiting It Out

TMJ pain is often dismissed as stress that will pass. But the structural pattern — head forward, neck loaded, jaw compensating — doesn't resolve on its own. Left alone, the clicking and clenching usually get more entrenched, not less.

Result: A pattern that deepens and becomes harder to reverse.

The Structural Cause of Most TMJ Pain

It starts above the jaw — in how your head sits on your neck.

Forward Head Posture

When your head drifts forward of your shoulders — from screens, driving, and desk work — the muscles under your jaw and at the base of your skull are pulled into constant tension. That tension changes the resting position of your jaw and forces it to work against a load it was never meant to carry.

Jaw Muscle Imbalance

The muscles that close and move your jaw are powerful, and when one side overworks — from one-sided chewing, clenching, or neck tension — the jaw stops tracking evenly. This uneven pull is what produces clicking, popping, and the feeling of the jaw catching or locking.

Neck and Skull-Base Tension

The jaw doesn't act alone. It shares muscles and nerves with the neck and the base of the skull, which is why TMJ so often arrives alongside neck pain and tension headaches. Until the neck is freed up, the jaw keeps getting pulled back into the same dysfunction.

Frequently Asked Questions — TMJ & Jaw Pain

Can TMJ be fixed without a night guard or surgery?
▼
In most cases, yes. A night guard protects your teeth but doesn't change why you clench, and surgery is rarely needed for typical TMJ dysfunction. Because most TMJ pain is driven by muscle imbalance and forward head posture rather than joint damage, restoring the underlying alignment usually resolves the pain without appliances or operations.
Why does my jaw click, pop, or lock?
▼
Clicking, popping, and locking happen when the disc inside the jaw joint isn't tracking smoothly as you open and close. This is almost always caused by uneven muscle tension pulling the jaw off its natural path — often driven by neck position and one-sided clenching or chewing. Balancing the muscles that control the jaw lets the joint move cleanly again.
Is my TMJ connected to my neck pain and headaches?
▼
Very often, yes. The jaw, neck, and base of the skull share muscles and nerves, so a jaw under constant tension frequently produces neck pain, tension headaches, and sometimes ear fullness or ringing. This is exactly why treating the jaw in isolation tends to fail — the neck posture driving the pattern has to be addressed at the same time.
Is virtual as effective as in-person for TMJ?
▼
Yes. TMJ work is highly teachable via video — Dr. Garrett can observe your head and neck posture and jaw movement, identify your pattern, and guide your corrective releases in real time. The tools you learn are movement-based, so they translate perfectly to a virtual setting.
I clench in my sleep. How can exercises help with that?
▼
Nighttime clenching is your nervous system discharging tension that's already built into your jaw and neck muscles during the day. When you lower that baseline tension — by correcting head position and releasing the overworked muscles — there's far less load for your body to grind out at night. Many clients find their clenching eases substantially once the daytime pattern changes.
