# Project Novak — the target plan

**Status:** internal founder working plan · 2026-08-19
**Target:** Novak Djokovic chooses to fly to San Francisco for Garrett’s care.
**Time horizon:** three years, through August 2029.

## Internal direction

Project Novak is Amari’s plan to bring Novak Djokovic under Garrett’s care.

From San Francisco, we will build a high-trust premium care business, become part of the serious tennis and performance conversation, and earn the relationships that let Novak’s team experience Amari firsthand.

The company and the target reinforce each other: the business creates the quality, reputation, relationships, and resources required to reach Novak; the Novak target directs that growth toward the highest level of performance.

## The rule: every path must lead somewhere

A Project Novak activity needs to create at least one of these within a defined window:

1. **A better care reputation** — a person with a demanding body has a real, accurately described experience of Amari.
2. **A stronger business** — paid care, retained clients, a repeatable premium acquisition path, or capacity beyond Garrett’s individual hours.
3. **A closer tennis/performance connection** — a trusted person who can responsibly introduce Garrett one step nearer to professional tennis or Djokovic’s performance circle.

If it makes none of those advances, stop. We are not spending ten years on the pickleball circuit or the North Korean table-tennis circuit merely because it is technically sport-adjacent.

## The two outcomes we are building simultaneously

| By August 2029 | What success looks like |
|---|---|
| **Novak path** | A credible, warm route reaches Djokovic or a genuinely relevant member of his performance circle, backed by care quality and a reason for them to take the introduction seriously. |
| **Business path** | Amari has a substantial premium-care business with repeatable demand, real client proof, trusted introducers, and a delivery model that does not depend solely on Garrett’s hours. |

Neither is allowed to consume the other. The business cannot become an excuse to stop pursuing Novak; the Novak pursuit cannot become an excuse to run an unserious business.

## Where we begin: body professionals

The first commercial wedge is **body professionals**: people whose bodies are part of their work and identity, who can make a premium-care decision, and who are trusted by other body-aware people.

Initial lanes:

1. **Independent trainers and movement coaches.** They are the clearest current fit and can become serious clients, observers of the work, and credible introducers.
2. **Tennis and racquet-sport professionals.** Coaches, teaching pros, and other serious racquet-sport people create a real tennis learning environment—not automatic access to elite tennis, but a deliberate first connection to it.

This is a starting wedge, not an identity prison. A later lane earns attention only if it improves the business or gives Amari a materially better connection to high-performance tennis.

## The actual route to Novak

We do not need to “know Novak” today. We need to build a chain of people who have experienced or observed the work, understand why it matters, and can make the next introduction responsibly.

```text
Exceptional care with demanding bodies
→ trusted local body / tennis professionals
→ respected performance connector
→ professional-tennis or elite-performance circle
→ a credible warm route to Novak’s team
→ Novak experiences Amari
```

The job is to identify and strengthen the next real link in that chain. It is not to assert that any local player, team, or facility already gives us access.

## First 90 days — choose, test, and earn permission

### 1. Select two lanes and two real hosts

Choose:

- one independent-trainer or movement-professional hub; and
- one tennis or racquet-sport hub.

A **host** is a real person or venue that can repeatedly see the work, give honest feedback, and appropriately introduce the right people. A spreadsheet of cold names is not a host.

**Definition of done:** each lane has a named host, a clear reason Amari belongs there, a repeatable first-experience format, and a date for the first 30-day review.

### 2. Run a small founder cohort

Keep it intimate enough to learn from every person. Garrett delivers excellent care. Eben records what is actually happening.

Track only:

- source and host;
- lane;
- the person’s own goal or concern;
- Assessment and follow-up completion;
- paid-care decision;
- return care;
- voluntary introductions;
- exact consent for any quote, story, or attribution; and
- whether this relationship advances the Novak chain.

No invented proof. No pressure for endorsements. No public association without clear consent.

### 3. Make the premium conversation real

The $5,400 24-session Practice is offered as a serious guided learning commitment, not a bundle of appointments. The conversation is earned by the individual’s experience and stays inside the current product truth:

- one-on-one, hands-on guided movement;
- the client actively participates;
- the work adapts as the person changes; and
- no promised performance, medical, or elite-athlete outcome.

The point is not to force every body professional into a Practice. It is to learn which people genuinely value it, why, and what language is accurate enough for the story to travel.

### 4. Ask for the next person naturally

After a meaningful experience:

> Who in your world asks a lot of their body and might be interested in experiencing this?

For a tennis/performance connector, the question is more specific:

> Who is one person you respect whose work or body would make them genuinely curious about this?

This is not a referral quota or incentive pitch. An introduction must be voluntary and appropriate.

### 5. Review weekly, decide every 30 days

Eben and Garrett review actual people and actual movement—not vanity metrics.

At day 30, 60, and 90, score each lane:

| Signal | What it proves |
|---|---|
| Completed first experiences | The invitation and host relationship are working |
| Return care / paid-care choice | Amari creates individual value in this lane |
| 12- or 24-session Practice decisions | The premium model fits the lane |
| Voluntary introductions | A real relationship network is forming |
| Named tennis/performance connectors | The Novak chain is becoming real |
| A warm next step from a connector | Access is advancing, not merely being discussed |
| Revenue by lane and source | The target pursuit is compounding the company |

### Decision rules

- **Double down:** a lane produces paid care and either voluntary introductions or a real high-performance/tennis bridge.
- **Keep as business-only:** a lane produces good revenue but no plausible Novak-chain progress. It can remain valuable, but it does not consume the targeted-access budget.
- **Repair:** people value a session but do not return or understand continued work. Improve experience, explanation, or fit before changing claims.
- **Exit:** after two 90-day cycles, a lane produces neither paid care nor warm next steps. Stop treating it as Project Novak work.
- **No expansion:** no broad paid campaign, team claim, celebrity outreach, referral-program build, GHL automation, or payment-path change until the relationship loop itself works.

## Months 4–12 — build proof and one serious bridge

### Revenue and care

Concentrate on the winning commercial lane. Create an exact path from introduction → first experience → paid Practice consideration → ongoing care → voluntary introduction. Build only consent-led proof, beginning privately if that is what the person prefers.

### Tennis access

Keep showing up where the chosen tennis host and their peers can observe the work. The year-one target is not a sponsorship or a famous player. It is a small number of people who can truthfully say, from experience, that Garrett’s work is worth introducing to someone serious.

By the end of year one, Project Novak should have:

- a healthy commercial lane that can fund the pursuit;
- a small base of demanding-body clients with real return behavior;
- at least one trusted tennis or performance connector; and
- a written, named hypothesis for the next link in the Djokovic route.

If we cannot name the next credible link, we do not pretend proximity is progress. We change the bridge strategy.

## Year 2 — become credible to the people who matter

Use the commercial base and the first trusted connectors to earn work with more demanding, better-connected bodies. The desired evidence is repeat care, discreet word-of-mouth, and a reputation for work that serious people choose again.

The target is one genuine bridge into professional tennis or a closely adjacent elite-performance network: a coach, performance practitioner, traveling trainer, player-development figure, or trusted advisor who understands both the person and the work. We do not need every sport; we need the right bridge.

Build destination-care readiness only when real demand supports it: scheduling, client experience, privacy, and capacity. Do not manufacture a luxury image before there is a practice to sustain it.

## Year 3 — make the attempt

By year three, the question becomes concrete:

- Who can make the warm introduction?
- Why is this specific person willing to make it?
- What has Garrett done that makes the introduction credible?
- What is the smallest, respectful invitation that makes it easy for Novak’s team to say yes or no?

The aim is an introduction to the person who can evaluate fit, not a gimmick or public stunt. If an appropriate invitation is available, make it. If it is not, retain the business and access assets, identify the missing link, and decide whether the next three years earn a rational continued attempt.

## Roles

| Owner | Accountable for |
|---|---|
| **Eben** | Keeps the target real; chooses lanes and hosts; measures revenue, access, and time; protects against dead-end activity; funds only work that earns a next step. |
| **Garrett** | Delivers extraordinary care; shows up repeatedly; earns trust; explains the work accurately; notices who is genuinely connected and who is merely impressive. |
| **Both** | Hold the monthly Project Novak review. Decide: deepen, redirect, or stop. No vague “we should keep networking.” |

## Explicit non-goals

- Being sport-adjacent for its own sake.
- Collecting famous names, team logos, or celebrity photos.
- Claiming affiliations or outcomes we have not earned.
- Spending years in low-leverage sports communities because they are available.
- Letting the business chase generic money without creating a better Novak route.
- Letting the Novak ambition consume resources without producing business or access evidence.

## First decision

Choose the initial two lanes and two candidate hosts. Then write the first named **Novak-chain hypothesis**:

> We believe this host can help Garrett earn trust with ___, who may plausibly connect to ___, which is one real step nearer to professional tennis / Djokovic’s performance circle.
If we cannot write that sentence honestly, choose a different host or classify the lane as business-only.
