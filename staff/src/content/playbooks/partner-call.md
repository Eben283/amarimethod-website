# Partner Call Playbook (v2)

Cold call to a golf pro, tennis pro, or fitness trainer in the Bay Area. Goal: book them for a free 60-min session at the SF studio.

**About v2.** Outline held up — Garrett's read after the 6/1 partner disco. Prose rebuilt from the transcript corpus. *Italics with a recording citation* = verbatim Garrett. ⚠️ = still inferred, replace once we have a real partner cold call recorded.

## Pre-call (30 sec)

Glance at their facility + most recent post or student win. Have one specific thing to mention so it doesn't feel like a script.

Geo: A = SF/Peninsula (primary), B = East Bay (secondary). Tier B with no shared context → LinkedIn connect first, not a call.

---

## 1. Open

⚠️ *No recording of Garrett opening a cold partner call yet. Working version below — swap after we have one.*

Job of the open: name yourself, name the studio, give them a clean out so they don't feel cornered.

> "Hey [name], it's Garrett from Amari Method in SF. We haven't talked before — is this an OK minute, or should I catch you later?"

One line, normal voice, then wait. If they say bad time → ask when's better, hang up. Don't push.

---

## 2. Get them talking about their students

Not a question list. One open question, then shut up.

A pro who teaches bodies all day has stories ready. You don't need to extract them — you need to ask one thing and not interrupt for 2 minutes.

Pick one:
- "What kinda stuff do you see in your students that you can't really get at as a coach?"
- "When somebody hits a plateau with you, what's usually the thing in their body that's in the way?"

**You're listening for the body issue THEY name.** You're going to use their exact words back in beat 3 — not yours.

---

## 3. The offer

You're comping a 60-min session because you want to be on their short list. Four pieces have to be in there; order can flex:

1. **What it is** — free 60 minutes at the SF studio. (Anchor: $225 if they were buying.)
2. **Why you're calling** — you want to be someone they can send students to when there's a body issue they can't reach as a coach. Use the issue they named in beat 2.
3. **Why the comp** — the only honest way to ask them to refer is if they've felt the work themselves.
4. **Out clause** — whether they refer after is up to them.

⚠️ Working delivery — looser than v1, but still authored:

> "Here's why I'm calling. I wanted to comp you a session at our studio — 60 minutes, normally $225. The reason: I want to be someone you can send students to when they've got something in their body you can't really get at as a coach. You said [tight hips / low back / whatever they said] — that's the kinda stuff I work on. The only way it makes sense to me to ask you to refer is if you've felt it yourself first. So come in, feel it, and then whether you want to send anybody is up to you."

Pause. Don't pile on.

---

## 4. What the work is (only if they ask)

Don't pre-explain. They tune out the second you reach for modality language. Wait until they ask "so what IS this?" Then answer in Garrett's actual voice — pick whichever of these lands in the moment:

- 🎙️ *"I help people wean themselves off of the medical model… these at-home protocols wean them off of needing anybody else."* — Questions 1-10
- 🎙️ *"The body is simple. The body is asking for balance. That is it."* — Questions 1-10
- 🎙️ *"Something in your body is working too hard because something else isn't working enough."* — Questions 1-10
- 🎙️ *"I'm a guide. I'm a facilitator. That's it."* — Recording 2474
- 🎙️ *"Michael Jordan needs a coach. Everyone has a blind spot."* — Questions 1-10

How that lands for THEM, in partner terms:

> "Their pain or whatever's stuck — I work on the imbalance underneath it. They learn to do it at home. They come back to you not just out of pain, but actually able to train. And you look like the coach who got them there — because you sent them somewhere that worked."

**Don't:**
- Say "Network Spinal," "somatic," "fascia" — modality lineage shuts it down
- Lead with "I'm a chiropractor" — 🎙️ *"As soon as I say chiropractor… most people, most chiropractors just crack crack crack, and they either like that or they hate that. Either way it's not what I do."* — Recording 2474
- Use the word "fix" — 🎙️ *"You don't fix a human body. You fix a kitchen sink. You fix a car."* — Questions 11-16
- Lead with credentials — true and dead

---

## 5. Book it on the call

If they're in, lock the calendar before they hang up. "I'll get back to you" mostly doesn't come back.

> "Cool. Let's get you on the calendar now. Mornings or afternoons?"

(wait)

> "I've got Tuesday 9, or Thursday 10:30. Which one?"

A/B beats "when are you free" — that makes them scan a whole calendar = friction = punt.

Once time is set:

> "Cool. Tuesday 9. Studio's at [address]. Wear something you can move in — don't need to bring anything. We'll spend the first 10 minutes talking about what your body's been doing, then get you on the floor. You'll feel different walking out."

⚠️ "You'll feel different walking out" — Garrett does say this about clients (🎙️ *"I see them walk differently. They sound different. Their voice gets deeper."* — Questions 1-10). Confirm he says it pre-session too.

Don't explain logistics before the time is set. It gives them a reason to stall.

---

## 6. Close

> "Great. See you [day/time]. Anything I should know before then?"

Confirm. Hang up. Don't re-pitch, don't add "feel free to call if anything changes."

## Objections — placeholder

⚠️ *Every objection script in v1 was authored using Voss/Hormozi frameworks, not transcribed from a real Garrett call. These are concepts, not lines. Real phrasing comes from real recordings.*

**"Let me think about it."** Don't push. Offer a placeholder slot they can cancel.

**"I already refer to someone."** Don't attack the incumbent. Ask how they handle cases the current person isn't a fit for.

**"Send me info."** Counterfeit yes. Offer a 10-min placeholder before sending anything.

**"What is this exactly / how does it work?"** Don't reach for modality. Use one of the 🎙️ lines from beat 4.

**"Why would my students need this — they already have me / a PT / a chiro?"** Garrett's actual stance:
- 🎙️ *"You can get pain relief with chiropractors and physical therapists, and of course painkillers. Of course. But you're not going to get balance."* — Questions 12-26
- 🎙️ *"I'm not looking for relief. I'm looking for function. Function, function, function."* — Questions 12-26
