# Partner Call Playbook (v3 — revenue roadmap)

Call with a golf pro, tennis pro, or fitness trainer in the Bay Area.

**Where the money actually is.** This call books a *free* session, so no money changes hands here. But the free session is the front door to two real revenue streams:
1. **The partner becomes a client.** They feel the work, have a breakthrough, and buy the series themselves (the 8-pack).
2. **The partner sends you clients.** Every client they refer is a potential series. That's the bigger number over time.

**So the one job on this call is simple: get the right person in to FEEL the work.** Ideally the owner or decision-maker, because the felt breakthrough is what sells the partnership — you don't have to. Garrett's real words for exactly this: 🎙️ *"My goal is that you or whoever is coming has a personal breakthrough for themselves. Because that would probably inspire them to want to talk about the partnership program."* — Recording 6/4 (Kwanua/PowerPlay).

**Not aggressive.** You're gifting something real and asking for nothing on the call but an hour of their time. No pressure, no overselling the partnership before they've felt anything.

Two starting points — pick the one that matches the call:
- **A. Warm** — they replied to a DM, or called you back (the default lately). Start at A1.
- **B. Cold** — calling out of the blue. Start at B1.

The middle and end (get them talking → offer → book) are the same either way.

*Italics with a recording citation = verbatim Garrett. ⚠️ = still inferred.*

## A1. Open — Warm

They expect this call. Confirm you're in the right conversation, then hand them the floor.

> "Hey [name] — thanks for booking. Saw your reply on [Instagram / LinkedIn]. What made you want to grab time?"

That last question is the move — they had a reason, let them tell you. (On the Kwanua call, Kelly opened with exactly what they wanted: *"we've been trying to find more partners to send our clients to."* That's the whole sale, handed to you. When that happens, skip ahead.)

**Do NOT use the cold disarm here** ("I know we haven't spoken before") — it reads as fake when they booked the time.

→ Skip to **the offer** if they've already named what they want.

## B1. Open — Cold

⚠️ *Still extrapolated — no recording of a true cold partner call yet.*

Disarm the "what does this person want from me" reflex. Name yourself, name the studio, give a clean out. Garrett's real cold-reach energy is light and human:

- 🎙️ *"I'm just calling to connect about your training business, if you have a minute to talk."* — voice profile
- 🎙️ *"My assistant just looks up personal trainers and hands me a list."* — voice profile (how he explains the cold reach, no pitch energy)
- 🎙️ *"I like the human to human thing. It seems rare these days."* — voice profile (he disarms by naming the awkwardness)

> "Hey [name], it's Dr. Garrett from Amari Method in SF. We haven't talked before — is this an OK minute, or should I catch you later?"

If bad time → ask when's better, hang up. Don't push.

→ Continue to **get them talking**.

## 2. Get them talking about their students

One open question, then shut up. A pro who teaches bodies all day has stories ready.

Pick one:
- "What kinda stuff do you see in your students that you can't really get at as a coach?"
- "When somebody hits a plateau with you, what's usually the thing in their body that's in the way?"

**Listen for the body issue THEY name** — you'll use their exact words back in the offer.

---

## 3. The offer — gift a session, aimed at the right person

You're comping a 60-min session because you want to be on their short list, and because the only honest way to ask for referrals is if they've felt the work. Garrett *gifts* it, he never sells it.

- 🎙️ *"Usually what I do is I would gift someone a session, whether it's you or the owner or whoever."* — Recording 6/4
- 🎙️ *"I'd love to gift you a session so you can get a sense of what I do."* — voice profile

Four pieces, order can flex:
1. **What it is** — free 60 minutes at the SF studio (normally $225).
2. **Why you're calling** — you want to be someone they can send students to for the body issue they can't reach as a coach. Use the issue they named.
3. **Why the comp** — the only honest way to ask them to refer is if they've felt it themselves.
4. **Out clause** — whether they refer after is up to them.

⚠️ Working delivery:

> "Here's why I'm calling. I'd love to comp you a session at our studio — 60 minutes, normally $225. I want to be someone you can send students to when they've got something you can't really get at as a coach. You said [tight hips / low back / whatever they said] — that's exactly what I work on. The only way it makes sense to me to ask you to refer is if you've felt it yourself first. So come in, feel it, and whether you send anybody is totally up to you."

**Revenue move: aim it at the decision-maker.** If you're talking to a manager or front desk (like Kelly, not Kwanua), the goal is to get the *owner* in to feel it — they're the one who'll sign on as a partner. Garrett did this naturally: gift it to "you or the owner or whoever," then made sure the owner would come experience it.

Pause. Don't pile on.

## 4. If they ask how the partnership works — the economics (verbatim)

Don't lead with this. But when they're interested and ask (Kelly did), Garrett lays it out plainly — and it's a real incentive, so say it with confidence:

- 🎙️ *"Our first initial session is $225. If you're in our partnership and you make that sale, you get 100 percent — you get the whole 225. If you just refer someone, then you get $50, because we still have to close the sale. But if you make the sale, you get the whole 225, and you just give us their info and we follow up and do the rest."* — Recording 6/4
- 🎙️ *"You could package it — say 10 sessions or a month plus structural relief with Dr. Garrett — and mark it up to 300 or 400. We don't care if you mark it up, we just ask that you don't go below 225."* — Recording 6/4
- 🎙️ *"Right now the partnership is just to keep your clients healthy so they train with you longer. And there's the financial incentive for you too."* — Recording 6/4 (he's honest it's one-way for now)

The booking link doubles as the partnership one-pager: 🎙️ *"On that link you can read up about the partnership program, and it'll just say book here — it'll say zero dollars, because it's your complimentary session."* — Recording 6/4.

## 5. What the work is (only if they ask)

Don't pre-explain — they tune out the second you reach for modality language. When they ask "so what IS this?", answer in his real voice:

- 🎙️ *"It's like that saying, feed a man for a day, or teach him how to farm for a lifetime. That's exactly what I do. We call it the Amari method, it has eight core protocols. The first phase is you experience it while I guide you, then little by little you learn to do it at home."* — Recording 6/4
- 🎙️ *"Any pain or tension just means you're out of balance. Something is working too hard because something else isn't engaging. It's that simple, and it's profound."* — Recording 6/4
- 🎙️ *"You become your own healer."* — Recording 6/4 (Kelly loved this line)

How it lands for THEM, in partner terms:

> "Their pain or whatever's stuck — I work on the imbalance underneath it. They learn to do it at home, they come back to you actually able to train, and you look like the coach who sent them somewhere that worked."

**Don't:** say "Network Spinal," "somatic," "fascia"; lead with "I'm a chiropractor" (🎙️ *"as soon as I say chiropractor, most people think crack crack crack, and that's not what I do"* — Recording 2474); use the word "fix"; lead with credentials.

## 6. Book it on the call

If they're in, lock the calendar before they hang up. "I'll get back to you" mostly doesn't come back.

> "Cool. Let's get you on the calendar now. Mornings or afternoons?"

(wait)

> "I've got Tuesday 9, or Thursday 10:30. Which one?"

A/B beats "when are you free" (scanning a whole calendar = friction = punt).

Once time is set:

> "Tuesday 9. Studio's at [address]. Wear something you can move in. We'll talk for the first 10 minutes about what your body's been doing, then get you on the floor. 🎙️ *You'll have a big state change that first time.*"

**Gatekeeper case:** if you're talking to someone who isn't the one coming in (a manager), send the one-pager link for them to forward to the owner, and name the session specifically: "I'll send the link — have [owner] grab a complimentary session, that's the part that matters."

## 7. After the free session — where the money actually is

The call got them in the door. The revenue happens *after* they've felt the work. Two moves, both in person, both grounded in the felt breakthrough:

### A. They become a client (the series)

Same logic as the discovery playbook: for chronic stuff the honest recommendation is the series, de-risked by the guarantee. Garrett recommends the 8-pack plainly:

- 🎙️ *"If you do an eight session package, which I definitely recommend, that brings it down to 162 a session, includes the first visit, and you get the living library videos."* — Recording 6/12
- Guarantee if they hesitate on cost: *"if you don't feel noticeable relief, we keep working until you do, at no extra charge."*

### B. "Who else?" — the referral motion

Right after they feel it, while it's real — this is the highest-leverage moment and it's easy to let it pass:

1. Lock the value: "fair to say we got that hip moving a lot better, and you felt it?" Get the yes.
2. Then ask and STOP: "who else on your roster keeps hitting a wall like that? I'd love to help them too." Say nothing, let them scroll their list.
3. Gently: "anyone else come to mind?" until they're tapped out.
4. Make it effortless: "can you intro me to [name] by text? I'll write it so you just forward it."
5. Close the loop yourself — nudge the next day, work each name as a lead.

### C. Set up the partnership

If they're inspired, walk them through the economics (Section 4) and get them in the program so referrals are tracked and they're paid.

## Objections

⚠️ *Concepts, not verbatim lines, except where cited.*

**"Let me think about it."** Don't push. Offer a placeholder slot they can cancel.

**"I already refer to someone."** Don't attack the incumbent. Ask how they handle cases the current person isn't a fit for — you want to be the second option for what doesn't respond.

**"Send me info."** Often a soft no. Offer a 10-min placeholder before sending anything — but for a warm gatekeeper, sending the one-pager link to forward to the owner is the right move (the Kwanua play).

**"What is this exactly / how does it work?"** Don't reach for modality. Use a 🎙️ line from Section 5.

**"Why would my students need this — they already have me / a PT / a chiro?"**
- 🎙️ *"You can get pain relief with chiropractors and physical therapists, and of course painkillers. Of course. But you're not going to get balance."* — Questions 12-26
- 🎙️ *"I'm not looking for relief. I'm looking for function. Function, function, function."* — Questions 12-26
