# Partner Call Playbook (v4 — cold call to comped session)

This playbook is for physical training partners: personal trainers, golf pros, and tennis pros.

There are two separate conversations:

1. **Act 1: Cold call** — book the 15-minute intro call.
2. **Act 2: Partner diagnosis call** — book the decision-maker’s complimentary studio session.

Do not pitch the economics or the protocols on the cold call. The cold call’s only goal is to sell the 15-minute meeting.

---

## Phase 0: Pattern-Interrupt Voicemail

Leave your name and number at the beginning, then again at the end.

> "Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] from Amari Method at [Your Phone Number]. Again, [Your Name] at [Your Phone Number]. I'm calling because we partner with [golf pros / personal trainers] in the Bay Area to help when your students hit a physical plateau that you can't really get at as a coach. We clear what's stuck structurally so your training lands better and holds longer. I'll follow up with a quick email, but feel free to call me back at [Your Phone Number]."

---

# Act 1: Cold Call — Book the 15-Minute Intro

Use a calm, downward-inflecting tone. Do not pitch the economics or explain the protocols.

## 1. Confirm and Disarm

> "Hey [Name], it's [Your Name] from Amari Method in SF. We haven't talked before — is this an OK minute, or should I catch you later?"

If it is a bad time, ask when is better and hang up. Do not push.

## 2. Ask the Break Question

Ask one open-ended question, then stop talking and let them answer.

### Personal trainers

> "When somebody hits a plateau with you, what's usually the thing in their body that's in the way?"

### Golf and tennis coaches

> "What kinda stuff do you see in your students that you can't really get at as a coach?"

## 3. Tie Their Answer to Amari

Listen for the specific physical limitation they name, such as tight hips or low back.

> "That is exactly why I'm reaching out. You see the pain, but structural bodywork just isn't your lane. The Amari Method helps clear that underlying [insert their specific issue] so your training lands better and holds longer."

## 4. Ask for the 15-Minute Call

> "I want to be respectful of your time today, but I'd love to see if we might be a good resource for your practice. Does it make sense to schedule a quick 15-minute call next week to see if there's a fit?"

# Act 2: 15-Minute Partner Diagnosis Call — Book the Studio Session

The prospect is now expecting this call. The one job is to get the right person into the studio to **feel the work**. You do not have to sell the partnership; the felt breakthrough sells the partnership.

## 1. Warm Opening (Minute 1)

Do not reuse the cold disarm. Confirm the prior conversation and hand them the floor.

> "Hey [Name] — thanks for making the time today. When we spoke briefly last week, you mentioned that your [golfers/tennis players/training clients] sometimes hit a wall because of [insert the specific issue they named on the cold call, e.g., tight hips]. What exactly does that look like when they get stuck?"

Listen carefully. Use their exact words in the offer.

## 2. Four-Part Gift Offer (Minutes 2–5)

Offer the complimentary 60-minute session. You are gifting it, never selling it.

> "Here's why I wanted to connect today. I’d love to comp you a session at our studio — 60 minutes, normally $225.
>
> I want to be someone you can send your students to when they've got something you can't really get at as a coach.
>
> You said your clients struggle with [tight hips/low back] — that's exactly what I work on. The only way it makes sense to me to ask you to refer is if you've felt it yourself first.
>
> So come in, feel it, and whether you send anybody is totally up to you."

## 3. Assumptive A/B Close (Minute 6)

Lock the calendar before they hang up. Use an A/B choice instead of asking them to scan their whole calendar.

> "Cool. Let's get you on the calendar now. Mornings or afternoons?"

After they answer:

> "Great. I've got Tuesday 9, or Thursday 10:30. Which one?"

Once set:

> "Tuesday 9. Studio's at [address]. Wear something you can move in. We'll talk for the first 10 minutes about what your body's been doing, then get you on the floor. You'll have a big state change that first time."

# Common Partner Questions

## “What is this exactly? Are you a chiropractor or PT?”

Do not reach for modality language, including fascia or chiropractor.

> "Any pain or tension just means you're out of balance. Something is working too hard because something else isn't engaging. It's that simple, and it's profound. The first phase is you experience it while I guide you, then little by little you learn to do it at home. You become your own healer."

## “What is the financial incentive for me?”

Do not lead with economics. If asked, lay it out plainly.

> "Our first initial session is $225. If you're in our partnership and you make that sale, you get 100 percent — you get the whole 225. If you just refer someone, then you get $50, because we still have to close the sale. You could package it into a coaching intensive and mark it up to 300 or 400. We don't care if you mark it up, we just ask that you don't go below 225."

## Gatekeeper: Gym Manager or Front Desk

Aim the complimentary session at the decision-maker, usually the owner.

> "I'll send the link — have [Owner's Name] grab a complimentary session, that's the part that matters."
