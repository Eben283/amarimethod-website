# Therapist Call Playbook — Learn the Moves, Then Use the Words

## The Two-Call Map

1. **Cold call:** earn the 15-minute intro call.
2. **Intro call:** get the therapist into the studio for a complimentary 60-minute session.

**Core lesson:** Do not try to sell a referral partnership before the therapist has experienced the work. The first sale is an hour in the studio.

---

## Lesson 1 — Make the voicemail feel personal and easy to return

**Why it matters:** The therapist needs to know who called and why before deciding whether to listen further. State your name and number first, then lead with the clinical challenge.

**Say:**

> "Hi April, it's Garrett from Amari Method at [Your Phone Number]. Again, Garrett at [Your Phone Number]. I was just on your website and loved your approach to helping clients reconnect with themselves. I'm calling because we partner with therapists in SF when a client's 'defense physiology' or physical trauma is quite literally blocking them from making an emotional breakthrough in your chair. We help them learn to become their own healer so your work lands better. I'll follow up with a quick email, but feel free to call me back at [Your Phone Number]."

# Act 1: Cold Call — Earn the 15-Minute Intro

## Lesson 2 — Be calm, direct, and honest about the cold contact

**Why it matters:** Therapists are especially attuned to tone. A calm, slow voice and an honest opener signal safety instead of a sales ambush.

**Say:**

> "Hi [Name], it's Garrett from Amari Method in SF. We haven't talked before — is this an OK minute, or should I catch you later?"

## Lesson 3 — Ask about the clinical impasse, not about Amari

**Why it matters:** The prospect needs to describe the challenge in their own language before Amari can be relevant.

**Say:**

> "I loved what your website said about helping clients develop deeper connections. As a therapist, how do you handle it when a client's physical tension or 'defense physiology' is quite literally blocking them from making an emotional breakthrough in your chair?"

**Do next:** Stop talking and let them answer.

## Lesson 4 — Position Amari as adjacent to their lane

**Why it matters:** You are not asking them to change how they practice. You are naming a physical layer that can sit alongside their therapeutic work.

**Say:**

> "That is exactly why I'm reaching out. You see the trauma, but structural bodywork just isn't your lane. We guide clients through a physical self-discovery process where they learn to pull themselves out of that fight, flight, or freeze state, so your therapeutic work lands better and holds longer."

## Lesson 5 — The cold call only earns the next conversation

**Why it matters:** Do not introduce the comped session yet. The request is simply a small, scheduled conversation.

**Say:**

> "I want to be respectful of your time between clients today. Does it make sense to schedule a quick 15-minute call next week to see if this might be a good resource for your practice?"

# Act 2: Therapist Intro Call — Book the Studio Session

## Lesson 6 — Reopen the exact problem they named

**Why it matters:** The second call should start where the cold call ended. This demonstrates listening and gives them space to make the clinical impact concrete.

**Say:**

> "Hi [Name] — thanks for making the time. When we spoke briefly last week, you mentioned that your clients sometimes hit a wall because [insert their exact words, e.g., their body is still bracing for a threat]. What exactly does that look like when they get stuck in a session with you?"

**Use a label:**

> "It sounds like that is incredibly frustrating for both of you."

## Lesson 7 — Offer a real experience with a real out clause

**Why it matters:** The complimentary session is not a disguised demand for referrals. The therapist should feel free to decide after they have experienced the work.

**Say:**

> "Here's why I wanted to connect today. I’d love to comp you a session at our studio — 60 minutes, normally $225.
>
> I want to be a trusted resource you can send your patients to when their body is trapped in that defense physiology.
>
> You mentioned your clients struggle with [insert issue] — that's exactly what we work on. But the only way it makes sense to me to ask you to refer your patients is if you've gone through this self-discovery process and felt it yourself first.
>
> So come in, feel the work, and whether you send anybody our way after that is totally up to you."

## Lesson 8 — Use an A/B close to make the gift real

**Why it matters:** A vague offer creates a vague answer. Two concrete choices turn interest into a session on the calendar.

**Say:**

> "Let's get you on the calendar so you can feel what this is all about. Do you prefer mornings or afternoons?"

Then:

> "Great. I've got Tuesday at 9:00, or Thursday at 10:30. Which one?"

Once booked:

> "Tuesday at 9:00. The studio is at [address]. Wear something comfortable. We'll talk for the first 10 minutes about what your body's been doing, and then get you on the floor. You'll experience a very beautiful state change that first time."

# Question Lessons

## “Is this like yoga or EMDR?”

**Lesson:** Acknowledge the philosophical overlap, then distinguish Amari by the client’s active participation.

**Say:**

> "There are overlaps in the philosophy of helping the nervous system realize the danger has passed. But what we do is teach the client how to actively address their own underlying muscular imbalances. We show their body how to rebalance itself so they aren't dependent on someone else to fix them."

## “How do the logistics work with my patients?”

**Lesson:** Make the referral process feel simple so the therapist can stay focused on their own work.

**Say:**

> "We make it incredibly seamless. You just send them through our referral portal. We don't bill insurance directly, but we can provide a superbill for them. You stay entirely in the loop on their physical progress, and we handle all the scheduling so you can stay focused on their mental health."
