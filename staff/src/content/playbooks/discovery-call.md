# Discovery Call Playbook — Learn the Moves, Then Use the Words

**Call outcome:** understand the client → name what is going on → recommend what they need → **book and take payment before the call ends.**

## The Call Map

1. **Open transparently** so they do not brace for a pitch.
2. **Let them describe the problem** and hear the gap in their own words.
3. **Name the pattern and stakes** so they understand why it has not changed.
4. **Recommend plainly and remove risk** with the guarantee.
5. **Book and pay while still on the call** so a ready yes becomes a real appointment.

---

## Lesson 1 — Transparency earns the right to lead

**Why it matters:** A person in pain expects to be sold to. State the order of the conversation immediately so they can relax and tell the truth.

**Say:**

> "Tell me what's going on. I'll listen. Then I'll tell you what I think, and what I'd recommend."

**Do next:** Stop. Let them talk. Do not fill the silence or add a longer introduction.

---

## Lesson 2 — The real problem is what pain is taking from them

**Why it matters:** Body parts alone do not create urgency. The gap becomes real when they name what the limitation is preventing them from doing.

**Listen for:**

- The exact body part and how long it has been happening
- What triggered it
- What they tried and why it stopped helping
- What they have changed, avoided, or lost

**Ask:**

> "How is this impacting what you care about? What can't you do anymore—whether that's riding your bike, or just getting out of bed without losing two hours to pain?"

**Do next:** Listen before you explain. The goal is their story, not an intake form.

---

## Lesson 3 — Give them a simple explanation for why the pattern persists

**Why it matters:** You are not diagnosing anatomy. You are giving them a clear, memorable way to understand the pattern behind what they described.

**Say:**

> "The reason you're in pain is because something in your body is working too hard. And the reason it's working too hard is because other things aren't working enough."

**If they ask why they need help:**

> "Michael Jordan had a coach. You can't see your blind spot, and you can't be in your own body. I'm your guide, I guide you through the protocols."

**If they mention painkillers:**

> "You're shooting the messenger. When you're out of balance, you should be in pain."

**Do next:** Connect the explanation back to their own stakes: what happens if this continues, and what becomes possible if it changes.

**Language rule:** Do not say “fix.”

> "My job is to get you back to balance, back to neutral. How you mess yourself up is up to you."

---

## Lesson 4 — Recommend one clear path; do not make them choose a menu

**Why it matters:** Chronic pain calls for a plain recommendation, not a list of packages that leaves the prospect to diagnose themselves.

**Say:**

> "Based on what you've told me, what I'd recommend is the 8-session series. It's $1,295, which works out to $162 a session including today, and it comes with the full video library."

**Do next:** Stop talking. Do not pre-discount, explain the price away, or rush to a smaller offer. Wait for their reaction.

---

## Lesson 5 — Price hesitation is a risk question

**Why it matters:** The guarantee makes it safer to choose the recommendation instead of defaulting down because the prospect is afraid of wasting money.

**Say:**

> "Come in, we find out what's actually causing your pain, and if you don't feel noticeable relief, we keep working until you do, at no extra charge."

**Do next:** Return to booking. The guarantee removes risk; it is not an invitation to negotiate the price.

---

## Lesson 6 — A ready yes must become a calendar appointment on the call

**Why it matters:** “I’ll book later” is not a booking plan. Friction, cost concerns, broken devices, and life intervene after the call.

**Say:**

> "I've got Tuesday at 4 or Thursday at 4:30—which works?"

> "Great, I'm texting you the secure payment link right now while we're talking. Let me know when it pops up."

> "Okay, go ahead and put your details in so we can lock in Tuesday at 4."

**Do next:** Stay on the line. Narrate the payment process, confirm payment, then repeat back the appointment time.

**Exception:** If payment creates friction, payment can wait until the first visit. The booking does not.

---

# Objection Lessons

## “Can we just start with one session?”

**Lesson:** A single is a real option, but it should not erase the series path.

**Say:**

> "We can start with the single session today for $225 if you'd rather feel it first. If it lands—and it will—the $225 rolls right into the series, so you're not paying for it twice."

**Do next:** If they book the single, tee up the upgrade at session 1 or 2. A 4-pack upgrade is $495 more; an 8-pack upgrade is $1,070 more.

## “I’ve tried everything—massage, PT, chiro—why would this be different?”

**Lesson:** Validate the effort, then distinguish participation from passive treatment. Do not argue about the other approaches.

**Say:**

> "You probably have tried a ton. But everything you tried was passive—it was done to you. This is you learning to unwind it yourself, so it actually holds."

## “I need to think about it.”

**Lesson:** “Think about it” usually hides the actual constraint. Surface it without arguing.

**Say:**

> "What's the biggest thing you'd be weighing?"

> "How on board is your partner with this?"

## “Do you take insurance or Medicare?”

**Lesson:** Answer the logistics directly, remove risk, then return to the appointment.

**Say:**

> "We don't bill it directly, but we provide a superbill so you can submit it for reimbursement. But here is my guarantee to you: come in, we find out what's actually causing your pain, and if you don't feel noticeable relief, we keep working until you do, at no extra charge."

**Do next:** Offer two appointment times.

---

## Final Lesson — Integrity is part of the close

Recommend what is right, remove the risk, and be willing to walk away when it is not a fit. If they decline for a real reason—timing, money, or partner—do not put them in a follow-up sequence. Send one personal note in 3–4 weeks.
