# Discovery Call Playbook (v4 — book and pay on the call)

15-minute call with someone in pain deciding whether to pay for a session.

## The Core Rule of the Call

The primary job of this call is to understand the client, name what is going on, recommend what they actually need, and **book the appointment and take payment on the call**.

A "ready yes" that is told to "go book it later" will almost always evaporate because life gets in the way. If you encounter friction, such as a broken computer or a cost worry, you can defer payment to the first visit, but never defer the booking.

---

## 1. The Transparent Opening (Minute 1)

**Goal:** State the order of the call immediately so the prospective client is not braced for a pitch.

> "Tell me what's going on. I'll listen. Then I'll tell you what I think, and what I'd recommend."

---

## 2. Discovery & The Gap (Minutes 2–8)

**Goal:** Let them talk and listen specifically for:

- Exact body parts
- How long it has been happening and what triggered it
- What they have tried and why it stopped helping
- What they care about that they cannot do anymore

Ask:

> "How is this impacting what you care about? What can't you do anymore—whether that's riding your bike, or just getting out of bed without losing two hours to pain?"

---

## 3. The Diagnosis & The Stakes (Minutes 8–11)

**Goal:** Reframe the problem and show the trajectory of their current state versus their desired future.

> "The reason you're in pain is because something in your body is working too hard. And the reason it's working too hard is because other things aren't working enough."

If they mention painkillers:

> "You're shooting the messenger. When you're out of balance, you should be in pain."

If they ask why they need you:

> "Michael Jordan had a coach. You can't see your blind spot, and you can't be in your own body. I'm your guide, I guide you through the protocols."

Reflect where they are headed if nothing changes and what they get back if they address it. Do not use the word “fix.”

> "My job is to get you back to balance, back to neutral. How you mess yourself up is up to you."

---

## 4. The Recommendation & Risk Reversal (Minutes 11–13)

**Goal:** Recommend the series plainly for chronic pain, state the price without softening it, then remove risk with the guarantee.

> "Based on what you've told me, what I'd recommend is the 8-session series. It's $1,295, which works out to $162 a session including today, and it comes with the full video library."

Stop talking. Do not pre-discount. Wait for their reaction.

If they hesitate on price or risk:

> "Come in, we find out what's actually causing your pain, and if you don't feel noticeable relief, we keep working until you do, at no extra charge."

---

## 5. Book and Pay on the Call (Minutes 13–15)

**Goal:** Give two options, text the link, and narrate the payment process so there is no dead air.

> "I've got Tuesday at 4 or Thursday at 4:30—which works?"

> "Great, I'm texting you the secure payment link right now while we're talking. Let me know when it pops up."

> "Okay, go ahead and put your details in so we can lock in Tuesday at 4."

Narrate the process, confirm the payment landed, and confirm their slot.

---

## Handling Core Objections

### “Can we just start with one session?”

> "We can start with the single session today for $225 if you'd rather feel it first. If it lands—and it will—the $225 rolls right into the series, so you're not paying for it twice."

If they book the single, the upgrade to a 4-pack is $495 more and an 8-pack is $1,070 more. Tee this up at session 1 or 2.

### “I've tried everything—massage, PT, chiro—why would this be different?”

> "You probably have tried a ton. But everything you tried was passive—it was done to you. This is you learning to unwind it yourself, so it actually holds."

### “I need to think about it.”

> "What's the biggest thing you'd be weighing?"

> "How on board is your partner with this?"

### “Do you take insurance or Medicare?”

> "We don't bill it directly, but we provide a superbill so you can submit it for reimbursement. But here is my guarantee to you: come in, we find out what's actually causing your pain, and if you don't feel noticeable relief, we keep working until you do, at no extra charge."

Then pivot immediately back to two times to book.

---

## When to Walk Away

Recommend what is right and remove the risk, but be genuinely willing to walk away when it is not a fit.

If they decline for a real reason—timing, money, or partner—do not put them in a follow-up sequence. Send one personal note in 3–4 weeks.
