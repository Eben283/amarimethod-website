# Partner LinkedIn First Message

Cold/cool outreach to a golf pro, tennis pro, or fitness trainer where the offer is a comped 60-minute session at the SF studio. Works as a LinkedIn DM, SMS, or email body. Use after they accept your connection request (300-char connection-note version below).

**Where this sits in the money path.** This is the *top* of the partner funnel. It doesn't make money by itself — its only job is to get a booked free session, which is the front door to the two real revenue streams (the partner becomes a client, and the partner sends you clients — see the Partner Call playbook). So the win isn't a friendly reply, it's a session on the calendar. Every line below is pointed at that.

**Not aggressive.** It names the ask honestly and hands them a clean out. The autonomy clause is what keeps the named ask from feeling like pressure.

---

## The template (LinkedIn DM / SMS / email body)

> Hey [first name], [your name] from Amari Method here in SF. Wanted to comp you a 60-minute session at our studio. I want to be someone you can refer clients to for the body issues you see but can't fix as a coach — hip rotation, low-back tightness, whatever's tight. The only way that makes sense to me is if you've felt the work yourself first.
>
> Most pros end up finding a few things in their own body too. Whether you decide to refer is up to you. Want me to send the booking link?

---

## Why each line lands

- **"Hey [first name], [your name] from Amari Method here in SF"** — natural DM opener, the way a person actually starts a message to a stranger.
- **"Wanted to comp you a 60-minute session at our studio"** — concrete offer with specific duration (no vague "full session" that leaves them wondering about commitment).
- **"I want to be someone you can refer clients to for the body issues you see but can't fix as a coach"** — names the ask plainly. The whole point of the comp IS to earn a referral relationship; saying so directly is the respect move. Hiding the ask triggers the "what's the catch" reflex.
- **Body-issue list** — *"hip rotation, low-back tightness, whatever's tight"* — concrete, ends loose with "whatever's tight" the way a real person trails off.
- **"The only way that makes sense to me is if you've felt the work yourself first"** — explains the comp as the path to the ask. Quiet conviction.
- **"Most pros end up finding a few things in their own body too"** — personal-breakthrough seed (the breakthrough is what converts them to a partner). Kept understated.
- **"Whether you decide to refer is up to you"** — autonomy clause. Honest autonomy now (because the ask was named), not hedged "no expectation" framing that reads as soft-dishonest.
- **"Want me to send the booking link?"** — drives to the one action that matters: a booked session. Plain question, not a hard close.

---

## The goal is a booked session, not a reply

A "sounds cool, I'll think about it" is not a win — it's a stall. When they reply with any interest, the move is to **send the booking link immediately and get the session on the calendar** (the same "book it, don't leave it to later" rule as the call playbooks). Then run the **Partner Call playbook** from the offer/booking section if they want to talk first.

- Reply with interest → "Love it — here's the link, grab any time that works: [link]. It's complimentary, takes about an hour, and you'll feel a real shift."
- They want to talk first → switch to the Partner Call playbook.

---

## What to vary per recipient

- **[first name]** — always personalize.
- **Body-issue list** — swap in items that match what their clients likely struggle with:
  - Golf: *"hip rotation and shoulder mobility, whatever's tight"*
  - Tennis: *"low-back tightness and rotation, whatever's stuck"*
  - General fitness: *"thoracic stiffness and ankle mobility"*

  Keep to two specific items + a loose trailing phrase.

---

## What NOT to change

- **The order** (opener → offer → ask + niche → why-comp-makes-sense → personal seed → autonomy → CTA)
- **The named ask** (*"I want to be someone you can refer clients to"*) — do not revert to hedged "no expectation" framing
- **The autonomy clause** (*"Whether you decide to refer is up to you"*) — this is what makes the named ask non-pressuring

---

## Connection request note (300 char max)

LinkedIn caps connection-request notes at 300 characters. The full template above is ~575 characters and won't fit. If they're a 3rd-degree connection, send this connection note first; once they accept, send the full DM above as message 1.

> Hi [first name] — [your name] here in SF. I run a somatic bodywork practice; most of my chronic-pain clients turn out to be golfers and tennis players. Would be good to be on each other's radar.

(186 chars — fits comfortably.)

---

## When NOT to use this template

- If the prospect is themselves a chronic-pain sufferer or already a known referrer → skip the for-your-clients framing, go straight to the offer.
- If sending via voicemail → use the call playbook script instead, not this written adaptation.
- If audience is a peer practitioner (DPT, chiropractor, massage therapist) → wrong category. Skip; they're not a referral partner, they're competitive overlap.
