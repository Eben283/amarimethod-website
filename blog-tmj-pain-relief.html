TMJ Pain Relief: Exercises to Stop Jaw Pain, Clicking & Tension

8 min read • February 2026 • By Dr. Garrett Hewstan

Your jaw clicks when you chew. Your temples throb. Opening your mouth wide causes shooting pain. TMJ disorder is affecting your quality of life, and you're wondering if surgery is your only option.

It's not. Most TMJ pain responds remarkably well to exercises, bodywork, and posture correction. You don't need surgery. You don't need to live with clicking jaw and chronic tension. In this guide, I'll explain what TMJ disorders actually are and how to fix them.

What is TMJ Disorder?

TMJ (temporomandibular joint) disorder is when your jaw joint becomes misaligned or the muscles around it become too tight. This creates:

  • Jaw clicking or popping (often worse when opening wide)
  • Jaw pain, especially when chewing
  • Limited jaw opening (can't open mouth fully without pain)
  • Temple headaches and migraines
  • Neck pain and shoulder tension
  • Ear pain (TMJ sits directly in front of ear)

The Root Cause: It's Usually Not Your Jaw

Here's what most dentists and doctors miss: TMJ disorder is usually caused by postural misalignment, not a jaw problem.

Here's how it develops:

Forward head posture (from screens, desk work) → neck misalignment → muscles around the jaw tighten → jaw joint becomes compressed → clicking, pain, and dysfunction develop.

You can spend thousands on dental treatments, bite guards, and surgery, and still have TMJ pain—because the real problem is your neck and shoulder posture, not your teeth.

TMJ Pain Relief: The Body Alignment Approach

1. Posture Correction (Addresses Root Cause)

Fix forward head posture, and jaw tension often improves dramatically. Screen height adjustment, hourly movement breaks, and chest-opening exercises (described in our tech neck guide) resolve many TMJ cases.

2. Jaw Relaxation Exercises

Masseter Release: Place fingers on your jaw muscles (feel them when you clench). Massage in circular motions, 2 minutes daily. This releases the muscle tension that compresses the jaw joint.

Jaw Relaxation: Let your jaw hang loose naturally (mouth slightly open). Don't force it. Practice this position 1-2 minutes daily, especially when stressed.

3. Cervical Mobility

Gentle Neck Rotation: Slowly turn your head to look over each shoulder. Hold 15 seconds. Do 5 reps per side. This releases neck muscles that affect the jaw.

4. Breathing Retraining

TMJ tension is often linked to shallow breathing. Practice deep belly breathing (4-second inhale, 6-second exhale) throughout the day. This activates parasympathetic nervous system and reduces jaw clenching.

When to Seek Professional Help

If TMJ exercises don't improve symptoms within 3 weeks, professional assessment helps. A specialist can identify if your TMJ is truly misaligned (vs. just tight muscles) and provide targeted correction.
