The point of the work

The table becomes optional.

The series is built so you don't stay dependent on it. You learn the protocols in session and take them home. Over time you need Garrett less, not more.

A follow-up now and then isn't a step backward. It's a check-in for a body that keeps living and working. You come in, your system resets, and you go back to running your own practice.

In practice

Light touch, on your schedule.

There's no ongoing package to buy and no standing appointment to keep. People reach for ongoing care in a few different ways, and none of them ask much of you.

When something flares

A single session to reset it.

An old area gets loud again, or a new strain shows up after a move or a hard week. One follow-up is usually enough to walk it back and remind your body what it already knows.

With the seasons

An occasional tune-up.

Some people come in every few months just to stay ahead of things. Not because anything is wrong, but because a short reset keeps the drift from adding up.

At home, daily

Five minutes on your own floor.

The Living Practice keeps the protocols in your hands between sessions. About five minutes a day is what holds the gains and keeps you off the table longer.

When you want a reset

A lighter entrainment.

Entrainments are the maintenance version of a session. Gentler, shorter in feel, and aimed at settling your system rather than working through something new.
