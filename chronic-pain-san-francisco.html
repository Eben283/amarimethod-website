Why nothing has ended it

Chronic pain means your body found a new balance. The wrong one.

Somewhere along the way, one part of your body took on work that used to belong to another part. Your body adapted, and the adaptation is what's hurting now.

01

You've tried the treatments. Not the pattern.

Physical therapy, massage, injections. They all work. They bring relief that lasts hours, days, sometimes weeks. Then the pain returns. That's not the treatment failing. It's a sign the treatment reached the symptom and not what's underneath it.

02

Pain that keeps returning is pattern pain

Pain that comes back to the same place again and again is your body telling you something is consistently overworking while something else has gone quiet. Until that pattern changes, the pain has a reason to come back.

03

What changes once the pattern does

Once Garrett identifies what's overworking and what's gone quiet, and brings your body back into a working pattern, the area in pain stops being asked to do all the work. Most clients feel a difference in their first session.

Your body isn't broken, it adapted to a load. Once that load moves off the one part doing all the work, the pain stops being needed.

FAQ

Common questions.

I've had this pain for years. Is this still for me?

+

Yes, especially if physical therapy, massage, and injections haven't ended it. Chronic pain that returns despite treatment usually means the treatment is addressing the symptom, not the pattern underneath it. Many of Garrett's clients had been managing pain for 10, 15, or 20 years before the pattern was finally addressed.

What conditions does this work for?

+

Lower back pain, neck pain, shoulder pain, hip pain, knee pain, sciatica, TMJ, plantar fasciitis, and most chronic conditions where the body has been carrying an uneven load for a long time. The mechanism is the same across all of them: find what's overworking, find what's gone quiet, and change the pattern.

What if my pain started with an injury?

+

Many chronic injuries are an old pattern plus an event. The injury is real, but the pain often persists because the pattern that made that area vulnerable is still there. Garrett works on the pattern. The injury heals on your body's own timeline once the load is changed.

What about pain medication?

+

The Amari Method doesn't replace medication directly. Most clients find their need for it reduces over time as the underlying pattern changes. Decisions about medication stay between you and your physician. Garrett's job is to change why your body is producing pain in the first place.

How is this different from physical therapy?

+

Most physical therapy strengthens what's weak and stretches what's tight. Garrett works upstream first, finding which structures should be doing work but aren't, and bringing them back online before adding strength. The movements can look similar. The sequence and the reasoning behind them are different. Clients who tried PT before often describe this work as more efficient and the change as more lasting.
